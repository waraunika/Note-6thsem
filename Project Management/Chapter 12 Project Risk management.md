# Understanding Risk
- a risk is a possible future event that may affect our project - positively (i.e. opportunity) or negatively (threat)
- risk is the possibility of loss or damage, it makes it harder to achieve the project's goal.
- risks are generally loss inducing and are uncertain
- a risk is an event that has some probability of occuring
- risk is also a joint function of risk = f( likelihood, impact )
## Types of Risk
1. Project Risk: occurs due to technical aspect of work or work product
2. Process Risk: occurs due to formation, processes of the project or methodologies used in the project
3. Product Risk or Technical Risk: affect the quality or performance or even implementation possibilities
4. Business Risks: affect the organization developing or procuring the software
5. Known risks: easily uncoverable risks
6. Predictable risks: extrapolated from past project experience
7. Unpredictable Risks: May or may not occur and are extremely difficult to identify
### Project Risk
- Project risk is an uncertainty that can have a negative or positive effect on meeting project objectives
- risks are possible in any project.
	- depends on the nature of risk and environment
- as the project progresses, risk and its potentiality do change
- risk evaluation should be done for events and necessary preparation have to be made
- customer's tolerance to risk must be considered
- Risks do have consequences
- Risk planning must be done for all kinds of risk (good or bad)
### Common Risks in IT projects
- market risk
- financial risk
- technology risk
- people risk
- structure/process risk

# Risk Management
- process involved with identifying, analyzing and responding to risk
- it includes maximizing the results of positive risks and minimizing the consequences of negative events
- it is often overlooked in projects
- many project problems can be reduced by using risk analysis.
- risk management procedures help in finding and avoiding problems,
- and also helps software project managers prevent surprises, improve negotiations, meet customer requirements and reduce schedule slips and cost overruns
## Terminology
- Risk Tolerance - amount of acceptable risk
- Risk Averse - someone that does not want to take risks
- Risk factors -
	- probability of occurrence
	- range of possible outcomes
	- expected timing of event
	- anticipated frequency of risk events from that source
- Residual Risk
	- the risks that remain after all the response strategies have been implemented
	- even though a stable hardware product may have been used on a project, there may still be a risk that fails to function properly
- Secondary Risks
	- the direct result of implementing a risk response
	- using the more unstable hardware may have caused a risk of peripheral devices failing to function properly.

# Risk Management Planning Process

- it is a part of Project Planning phase
- it is all about
	- Planning
		- planning risk management
		- identifying risks
		- performing qualitative and quantitative risk analysis
		- planning & implementing risk responses
	- Controlling and monitoring
		- controlling risk
- risk management plan decides how we handle risk, in the form of loop:
	- Risk Identification -> (find them)
	- Qualitative risk analysis -> (shift)

	- Risk Response Planning -> (decide actions)
	- Risk monitoring and control -> (act and measure)
	- Risk Identification

## Risk Identification
- part of project planning phase
- identifying risk is the process of understanding what potential events might hurt or enhance a particular project
- the main output of the risk identification process is a list of identified risks and other information needed to being creating a risk register
### Risk Register
- it is a tool in risk management an dproject management
- used to identify potential risks in a project or an organization
- contains the outcomes of the risk management processes as they are conducted
- Risk register contains
	- list of identified risks
	- list of potential responses
	- Root causes of risk
	- updated risk categories
- Risk database is the electronic from of risk register
- checklist could be used for the identification process
#### Includes:
1. Risk Identification ID: Risk Name or ID
2. Risk Description: A brief explanation of the risk
3. Risk categories: There are many risk categories that can impact a project such as schedule, budget, technical or external risks
4. Risk analysis: The purpose of risk analysis is to determine the probability and impact of a risk. you can either do a qualitative or quantitative risk analysis
5. Risk probability: need to estimate the likelihood of each risk and assign a qualitative or quantitative value
6. Risk priority: the risk priority is determined by assigning a risk score to each risk, which is obtained by multiplying the risk impact and probability values. if you're using qualitative measurements, you'll need to prioritize risks with the highest impact and highest probability
7. Risk Response: each risk needs a risk response to mitigate its effect on your project. those risk responses are also documented in a risk response plan
8. Risk ownership: each risk needs to be assigned to a team member who becomes a risk owner. the risk owner is responsible for deploying the appropriate response and supervising it

### Risk identification Tools & Techniques
1. Brainstorming
	- technique by which  a group attempts to generate ideas or find a solution for a specific problem by collecting ideas without thinking and judgement
	- don't overuse - can lead to groupthink and fewer ideas
2. The delphi  technique
	- lots of similarities to brainstorming
	- people participated in this technique don't have to be located in the same place and can participate anonymously
	- we can use email to facilitate this technique easily.
	- the technique is used to make decisions about complex issues based on individual opinions
	- a group of experts write down and share their thoughts about a problem with a facilitator
	- each expert's view is compiled into a summary report by the facilitator.
3. Interviewing
	- fact finding technique for collecting infoomation face-to-face, phone, e-mail or instant messaging discussions
	- interviewing people with similar project experience is an important tool for identifying potential risks
	- very useful technique to collect risk
4. SWOT analysis
	- SWOT analysis is the process of examining the project from each of teh characteristic's point of view
	- helps to identify the broad negative and positive risks that apply to a project
	- SWOT analysis can also be used during risk identification
	- Strengths, Weakness, Opportunities and Threats
5. Nominal Group Technique
	- Another technique similar to delphi technique
	- participants are grouped in same room, given pen and paper
	- they are asked to write down what risks they think the project faces
	- the papers are given to facilitators and stick to the wall or white board
	- the risks are then reviewed and prioritized
	- at the end, we have a complete list of risks
## Risk analysis
- process of analyzing the identified risks, their probability of occurrence and their impacts
- results are stored in an updated risk register containing the priority of risk to be dealt with
- two methods of analysis: qualitative and quantitative risk analysis
### Qualitative
-  part of planning process group
- qualitative risk analysis is the process of evaluating the impact and possibility of identified risks
- prioritizes risks according to their potential effect on the project
- risks are analyzed on qualitative factors only
- tools and techniques include:
	- probability/impact matrices
	- the top ten risk item tracking
	- expert judgement
- a watch list is a list of risks that are low priority, but are still identified as potential risks.
### Quantitative
- part of the planning process group
- quantitative risk analysis is the process of determining numerically the probability of each risk and its consequences of project objectives
- identifying risk requires the most attention
- this process quantifies risk exposure and determines the size of cost and schedule contingency reserves that may be needed.
#### Calculating risk exposure
- determine probability of occurrence for each risk component
- determine the impact for each component based on the criteria shown from the table
- prepare a risk table and analyze the result i.e. overall risk exposure
RE = P * C; where P is the probability and C is the cost to the project, should the risk occur

## Modeling Techniques
1. Sensitivity Analysis
	- sensitivity analysis is a technique used to show the effects of changing one or more variables on an outcome
2. expected monetary value
	- EMV is a project management metric used in risk analysis for determining the overall resource plan required for a project plan
	- when you make a plan, it can go better or worse than you expected.
	- e.g, decision tree and risk exposure methods
		-  Decision Tree
			- a decision tree, also known as decision tree analysis, is a diagram that will help to identify outcomes due to a collection of related choice
			- used for EVM study.
3. Monte Carlo Approach
	- mathematical technique, which is used to estimate the possible outcomes of an uncertain event
	- includes simulation and modeling
		- simulation uses a representation or model of a system to analyze the expected behavior or performance of the system.
	- steps for analysis:
		1. Evaluate the range for the variables being considered
		2. Determine the probability distribution of each variable
		3. for each variable, select a random value based on probability distribution
		4. run a deterministic analysis or one pass through the model
		5. repeat steps 3 and 4 many times to obtain the probability distribution of the model's results
4. Tornado Analysis
	- types of simulation modeling
	- there are different types of charts used in project management
	- the tornado diagram is a special bar chart that is used in sensitivity analysis
	- the sensitivity analysis is a modeling technique that determines which risks have the most impact on the project.
	- the tornado diagram is used to compare the importance (relative) of different variable
	- the tornado diagram is used to display the sensitivity analysis and compare relative importance and impact of variables with a high degree of uncertainty and stability
	- this particular bar chart has tornado like form because the risk data categories are arranged vertically from largest to smallest on horizontal bars.
## Risk Response Planning
- part of planning process group
- after identifying and quantifying risks, we must decide how to respond to them
- it is the process of creating options to improve chances to improve chances and cut down any obstacles to project goals, thus lowering total risk
- typical risk resolution actions are:
	- research and review
	- eliminate and review
	- reduce and protect
	- accept and protect
## Strategies for Threat (Negative risks)
- risk avoidance - eliminating a specific threat, usually by eliminating its causes
- risk acceptance - accepting the consequences if a risk occurs
- risk transference - e.g., shifting the consequences of a risk and responsibility for its management through outsourcing
- risk mitigation - reducing the impact of a risk event
## Strategies for Positive Risks (Opportunities)
- Risk exploitation - doing whatever we can to make sure the positive risk happens
- Risk sharing - allocating ownership of teh risk to another party so that they can better realize the opportunity
- risk enhancement - changing the size of opportunity by identifying, increasing probability and maximizing key drivers of the positive risk
- Risk acceptance - the project team does not take any actions towards a risk
- risk escalation - notifying a higher level authority
## Strategies for both Positive and Negative Risks
- Risk Acceptance
	- allowing the consequences should a risk occur in a known and controlled manner
	- live with consequences
- Contingent response strategies
	- develop contingency plan
	- create contingency allowance procedure
	- plan for alternative development
## Risk Monitoring and Controlling Process
- process of keeping track of identified risks, monitoring residual risks, executing risk plans and evaluating the effectiveness of reducing risks
- monitoring risks involves knowing their status
- controlling risks involves carrying out the risk management plan as risk occur
