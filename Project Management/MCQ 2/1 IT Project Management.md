## 0.1 Executive Summary
- detailed analysis of IT project failures based on a meta-retrospective study of
- **99 retrospectives conducted in 74 organizations over seven years** (1999-2006). 
- The research, led by Professor R. Ryan Nelson at the University of Virginia, 
- examined projects ranging from several hundred thousand dollars to multi-billion dollar mission-critical applications. 
- The study involved **502 working professionals**, each with an average of over 10 years of experience, 
	- who participated in the Master of Science in the Management of Information Technology (MS MIT) program.
- The key findings reveal that 
	- **45% of classic mistakes are process-related** 
	- **43% are people-related**
	-  **12% are product or technology-related**.

# 1 Part 1: Ten Infamous IT Project Failures
This section provides detailed case studies of the most heavily cited IT project failures, each exceeding $100 million in losses.
## 1.1 Internal Revenue Service (IRS) - Business Systems Modernization
| **Aspect**        | **Details**                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Project**       | Business Systems Modernization                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Launched**      | 1999                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Objective**     | Upgrade the agency's IT infrastructure and more than 100 business applications                                                                                                                                        |
| **Budget**        | $8 billion                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **What Happened** | The IRS assembled a "star-studded team of vendors" but mistakenly believed the project would manage itself. The agency's ability to collect revenue, conduct audits, and pursue tax evaders was severely compromised. |
| **Significance**  | Considered by some to be "the most expensive systems development fiasco in history," with delays costing the U.S. Treasury tens of billions of dollars per year.                                                      |
| **Primary Cause** | Management capabilities were overwhelmed by project complexity; failure of both vendor and client management.                                                                                                         |
## 1.2 Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) - Advanced Automation System (AAS)
| **Aspect**                   | **Details**                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Project**                  | Advanced Automation System (AAS)                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Objective**                | Modernize the nation's air traffic control system                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Original Estimate**        | $2.5 billion                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Original Completion Date** | 1996                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Actual Cost**              | $2.6 billion spent, with nearly $1.5 billion completely wasted                                                                                                                                                   |
| **What Happened**            | Numerous delays and cost overruns were blamed on both the FAA and primary contractor, IBM. The project aimed for "seven nines" reliability (99.99999%), an extremely ambitious goal that contributed to failure. |
| **Participant Quote**        | "It may have been the greatest failure in the history of organized work."                                                                                                                                        |
| **Primary Causes**           | Unrealistic reliability goals; contractor failure; poor requirements determination.                                                                                                                              |
## 1.3 Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) - "Trilogy" Project

# 5 Part 5: Best Practices to Avoid Classic Mistakes
This section provides detailed recommendations for avoiding the top seven classic mistakes (those occurring in at least one-third of projects).
## 5.1 Avoiding Poor Estimation and/or Scheduling (54%)
**The Estimation Process:**
- Sizing or scoping the project
- Estimating effort and time required
- Developing a calendar schedule considering resource availability, technology acquisition, and business cycles
**Benefits of Accurate Estimates:**
- Fewer mistakes
- Less overtime, schedule pressure, and staff turnover
- Better coordination with non-development tasks
- Better budgeting
- More credibility for the project team
### 5.1.1 Best Practices:
| **Practice**               | **Description**                                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Developer-based estimates  | Those who will do the work should estimate it.         |
| Modified Delphi approach   | Iterative consensus-building among experts.            |
| Historical data            | Use data from past projects.                           |
| Algorithms                 | COCOMO II and other estimation models.                 |
| Estimation software        | QSM SLIM-Estimate, SEER-SEM, Construct Estimate.       |
| Estimate-convergence graph | "Cone of uncertainty" with narrowing ranges over time. |
## 5.2 Capital One's Approach (Multipliers by Phase):
| **Phase**                      | **Cushion** |
| ------------------------------ | ----------- |
| Feasibility phase (beginning)  | 100%        |
| Definition phase               | 75%         |
| Design phase                   | 50%         |
| Construction phase (beginning) | 25%         |

**Four Valuable Approaches:**
1. **Timebox development** - Shorter, smaller projects are easier to estimate.
2. **Work breakdown structure** - Helps size and scope projects.
3. **Retrospectives** - Capture actual data for future estimates.
4. **Project Management Office (PMO)** - Maintain repository of project data over time.
## 5.3 Avoiding Ineffective Stakeholder Management (51%)
**Stakeholder Worksheet and Assessment Graph:**  
Three-dimensional mapping of:
- Stakeholder power (influence over others and direct control of resources)
- Stakeholder level of interest
- Stakeholder degree of support/resistance
### 5.3.1 Best Practices:
| **Practice**                           | **Description**                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Stakeholder worksheet/assessment graph | Identify stakeholders most in need of attention.      |
| Communication plans                    | Structured approach to stakeholder communication.     |
| Project Management Office (PMO)        | Centralized oversight.                                |
| Portfolio management                   | Recognize stakeholder roles across multiple projects. |
## 5.4 Avoiding Insufficient Risk Management (47%)
**Risk Management Process:**
1. Risk identification
2. Risk analysis
3. Risk prioritization
4. Risk-management planning
5. Risk resolution
6. Risk monitoring
### 5.4.1 Best Practices:
| **Practice**                      | **Description**                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Prioritized risk assessment table | Document and rank risks.                                            |
| Top-10 risks list                 | Actively manage the most critical risks.                            |
| Interim retrospectives            | Conduct during, not just after, project.                            |
| Risk officer                      | One person plays "devil's advocate" to identify potential failures. |
## 5.5 Avoiding Insufficient Planning (39%)
**Example of Failure:** A high-end leather accessories retailer proceeded with a major website upgrade just before Christmas without a formal business case analysis. Results included:
- Clear roles and responsibilities never established
- Resource battles common
- Kickoff delayed
- Project policies never fully developed
- Project languished until after holiday season
### 5.5.1 Best Practices:
| **Practice**                       | **Description**                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Comprehensive project charter      | Formal approval and scope definition.    |
| Clearly defined project governance | Decision-making structure and authority. |
| Portfolio management               | Coordinate across multiple projects.     |
## 5.6 Avoiding Shortchanged Quality Assurance (37%)
**The Problem:** When projects fall behind schedule, testing and training are often cut first.
**Example (U.S. Military Project):**
- Time planned for internal testing too short
- Unit tests dropped as deadline approached
- Integration testing came too late for regression testing
- Result: When feature-complete milestone reached, system too buggy to release for several more months
**The Cost:** Shortcutting one day of quality assurance early in a project is likely to cost **3 to 10 days downstream**.
### 5.6.1 Best Practices:
| **Practice**                            | **Description**                                      |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Agile development                       | Iterative approach with continuous testing.          |
| Joint Application Design (JAD) sessions | Collaborative requirements and design.               |
| Automated testing tools                 | Efficient, repeatable testing.                       |
| Daily build-and-smoke tests             | Complete build daily with basic functionality tests. |
**Benefits of Daily Build-and-Smoke Tests:**
- Reduces likelihood of low quality
- Reduces likelihood of unsuccessful integration
- Reduces likelihood of poor progress visibility
## 5.7 Avoiding Weak Personnel and/or Team Issues (37%)
**Problem Example:**
> "One of the key problems during the development phase of the project was the relatively low skill level of the programmers assigned to the project. The weak programming skills caused schedule lapses, poor quality, and eventually caused changes in personnel."

**Trend:** Increasing problems with distributed, inter-organizational, and multi-national teams (1999-2006).
**Distributed Team Challenges:**
- Reduction in face-to-face team meetings
- Time-zone barriers
- Language and cultural issues
**Best Practice:** **Colocation** - even when it requires sending staff to a foreign country for an extended period.
## 5.8 Avoiding Insufficient Project Sponsorship (36%)
**The Problem:** In some cases, support was lacking from the start. In others, the key project sponsor departed midstream, leaving a void never properly filled.
**Key Lesson:** Identify the right sponsor (typically the owner of the business process) from the very beginning, secure commitment within the project charter, and manage the relationship throughout the life of the project.
**Positive Example:**
> "There were CEOs on [project status] calls all the time. Nobody wanted to be written up in the 'Wall Street Journal'. That was what motivated people to change. Fear that the stock price would get hammered and fear that they would lose too much business."

**Positive Example of Cancellation:** One change in business sponsor led to the overdue cancellation of a runaway project, saving the U.S. Army and American taxpayers money. The review team concluded this project was a **"successful failure."**

# 7 Part 7: Leveraging IT Project Retrospectives
## 7.1 Benefits of Meta-Retrospective Analysis
1. **Pattern Recognition:** Uncover patterns of practice across projects and over time.
2. **Organizational Learning:** Higher-level learning than individual project postmortems.
3. **Long-term Benefits:** Accumulate knowledge over time.
4. **Increased Business Value:** Apply lessons to future projects.
## 7.2 Recommendations for PMOs
1. **Focus Education and Training** first on the areas where mistakes are most common (estimation, stakeholder management, risk management).
2. **Institute Best Practices** that address these shortcomings.
3. **Use the Matrix** to cross-reference best practices with classic mistakes.
4. **Proactive Identification:** At project front end, identify problems most likely to arise and prioritize project-specific best practices.
## 7.3 Example Applications
| **Project Characteristic**    | **Recommended Best Practice**                 |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Politically charged project   | Stakeholder assessment and communication plan |
| Ill-defined requirements      | Agile development                             |
| Heavy user involvement needed | Agile development, JAD sessions               |

# 8 Part 8: Conclusion
> "Insanity: doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results." — Albert Einstein

**Key Takeaways:**
1. **Focus on People and Process:** 88% of classic mistakes are people or process-related. Technical expertise alone is insufficient.
2. **Top 3 Priorities:** Better estimation and scheduling, stakeholder management, and risk management could have significantly improved the majority of projects studied.
3. **Estimation Trends:** While cost estimation has improved, time estimation has gotten worse.
4. **Proactive Best Practices:** The proactive and well-informed use of best practices is the best way to steer clear of classic mistakes and avoid becoming an infamous failure.
5. **Continuous Learning:** Conducting retrospectives and aggregating findings across projects enables organizational learning and continuous improvement.
