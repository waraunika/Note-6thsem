# 1 Advantages of project management
| Advantage                        | Description                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Increased Control**            | Enhanced oversight of financial, physical, and human resources throughout the project lifecycle.          |
| **Improved Customer Relations**  | Better communication, transparency, and delivery reliability build trust and satisfaction.                |
| **Higher Quality Outcomes**      | Structured quality planning and control processes ensure deliverables meet specifications.                |
| **Enhanced Reliability**         | Consistent processes lead to more predictable and dependable solutions.                                   |
| **Increased Profit Margins**     | Efficient resource utilization and reduced waste directly improve profitability.                          |
| **Improved Productivity**        | Clear roles, processes, and coordination eliminate duplication and streamline work.                       |
| **Better Internal Coordination** | Cross-functional collaboration improves as communication channels and responsibilities are clarified.     |
| **Higher Work Morale**           | Clear goals, recognition of achievements, and effective leadership boost team motivation.                 |
| **Shorter Development Time**     | Structured planning, scheduling, and control techniques (e.g., CPM, PERT) compress timelines.             |
| **Lower Costs**                  | Proactive cost management, risk mitigation, and efficient resource allocation reduce overall expenditure. |
# 2 Project management context as per PMI
- According to PMI, subdividing a project into different phases decreases uncertainty.
- Each project phase is marked by the completion of one or more **deliverables**.
- deliverables are:
	- **Tangible or visible:** They represent concrete progress.
	- **Verifiable:** They can be reviewed, tested, and approved against defined criteria.

## 3.1 Project life cycles
| Phase                             | Key Activities                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Start-Up (Initiation)**      | - Define purpose and strategic fit<br>- establish objectives<br>- draft scope<br>- create terms of reference<br>- develop draft schedule and budget estimate<br>- obtain approval to proceed.                                                        |
| **2. Planning**                   | - Finalize scope<br>- select team members<br>- plan deliverables<br>- develop quality plan<br>- establish baseline schedule<br>- create risk and issues registers<br>- finalize business case<br>- develop communication plan<br>- obtain approvals. |
| **3. Execution (Implementation)** | - Produce key deliverables<br>- monitor and control progress<br>- manage quality, time, cost, and risk<br>- resolve issues<br>- manage changes<br>- report status<br>- maintain communications.                                                      |
| **4. Close-Out (Closure)**        | - Celebrate achievements<br>- close contracts<br>- gather team feedback<br>- make recommendations for future actions<br>- conduct post-implementation review<br>- formally close the project.                                                        |

### 3.1.2 Characteristics of Project Life Cycle
| Characteristic             | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sequential Phases**      | Projects should pass through each defined phase, with each phase building upon the previous one.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Phase-End Reviews**      | The conclusion of each phase is marked by a review of key deliverables and project performance (often called a "phase gate" or "kill point").                                                                                                                                           |
| **Cost & Staffing Levels** | Cost and staffing levels are **low at the start**, **higher toward the middle/end** as work intensifies, and **drop rapidly** as the project closes.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Risk & Uncertainty**     | Risk and uncertainty are **highest at the start** of the project and progressively decrease as more information becomes known and deliverables are completed.                                                                                                                           |
| **Stakeholder Influence**  | The ability of stakeholders to influence the final characteristics of the project's product and its final cost is **highest at the start** and gets **progressively lower** as the project continues. Changes become more expensive and difficult to implement later in the life cycle. |

## 3.2 IT Product Development Life cycle
- The SDLC is a product development framework that describes the phases involved in developing and maintaining information systems. System development projects can follow two broad categories of models:
#### 3.2.1.1 Predictive (Plan-Driven) Models
- **Characteristics:** The scope of the product can be clearly articulated at the start; schedule and cost can be predicted with reasonable accuracy.
- **Approach:** Requirements are defined upfront, and the project follows a sequential or structured plan.
- **Examples:**
    - **Waterfall:** Linear, sequential approach where each phase must be completed before the next begins.
    - **Spiral:** Iterative model combining prototyping with risk analysis; each cycle refines the product.
    - **RAD (Rapid Application Development):** Focuses on rapid prototyping and quick feedback loops.
#### 3.2.1.2 Adaptive (Change-Driven) Models
- **Characteristics:** Products are mission-driven and component-based; requirements evolve through iterative cycles.
- **Approach:** Uses time-based cycles (iterations or sprints) to deliver working increments of the product on a regular cadence.
- **Examples:**
    - **Agile:** A broad philosophy emphasizing flexibility, collaboration, and customer feedback.
    - **Scrum:** An Agile framework using fixed-length sprints and cross-functional teams.
    - **Extreme Programming (XP):** Focuses on technical excellence, continuous testing, and frequent releases.
### 3.2.2 Comparison
|Aspect|Predictive (Waterfall)|Adaptive (Agile/Scrum)|
|---|---|---|
|**Requirements**|Defined upfront, fixed|Emergent, evolving|
|**Scope**|Fixed|Flexible|
|**Schedule & Cost**|Estimated upfront, relatively fixed|Re-estimated iteratively|
|**Customer Involvement**|Primarily at phase ends (e.g., requirements, testing)|Continuous throughout the project|
|**Delivery**|Single final delivery|Frequent, incremental deliveries|
|**Best Suited For**|Well-understood, stable requirements|Complex, uncertain, or rapidly changing requirements|
## 3.3 Product life cycle and project life cycle
| Aspect           | Project Life Cycle                                                        | Product Life Cycle                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**   | The series of phases a project passes through from initiation to closure. | The series of stages a product passes through from conception to retirement.                                                                                                        |
| **Scope**        | Focuses on the work required to create the product.                       | Focuses on the product itself across its entire existence.                                                                                                                          |
| **Duration**     | Temporary; ends when the project closes.                                  | Ongoing; continues beyond the project's closure.                                                                                                                                    |
| **Application**  | Applied to all projects, regardless of the product being produced.        | Varies considerably based on the nature of the product (e.g., software, hardware, consumer goods).                                                                                  |
| **Relationship** | One or more projects can exist within a single product life cycle.        | Most IT systems are developed as a **series of projects** over the product's life (e.g., initial development project, followed by enhancement projects, then maintenance projects). |
