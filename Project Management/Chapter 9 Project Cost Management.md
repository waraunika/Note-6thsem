# Basics
- Cost management is the process of planning and managing the budget of a business or project.
- in the case of a project, it helps the project manager to estimate what the project will cost and set controls to reduce the chances of the project going over budget
## Cost
- Cost refers to a resource spent in order to accomplish a particular goal
- costs are usually measured in monetary units.
### Types
1. Direct cost:
	- cost of labor, resource, machinery
2. Overhead Cost
	- cost of building, rent, maintenance, insurance, vacation
3. Administrative Cost
	- coat due to management expenses, HRM and HRD activities
## Project Cost
- cost of running a project
- it primary involves labor cost, resource cost and special machinery cost
- total sum of these 3 cost
- if a company wants to launch a new project then it must allocate human resources, machinery and other resources to it.
- the overall cost of these materials is project cost
## Product Cost
- cost involved in manufacturing a product
- it involves various costs such as material cost, manufacturing cost and overhead cost.
- it is also known as sales price.
- material cost + manufacturing cost + overhead cost = sales price
- overhead cost = project other overhead costs + project cost + profit
# Cost Estimating
- Cost estimating is the process of creating an estimate for the price of the resources required to finish the project's operations
- cost estimating also includes identifying and considering cost alternatives
- cost estimating process is a part of "Project Planning phase"
## Tools and Techniques
### Top Down Estimates
- Aka analogous estimates
- is based on top down approach
- makes comparison with other projects
- is less accurate than other tools
### Bottom-up estimates
- activity based costing
- also based on WBS
- more accurate than other tools
- creates a detailed estimate of each work components
- would be better for controlling for cost
### Three-point estimates
- involves estimating the most likely, optimistic, and pessimistic costs for items
- use formula like PERT weighted average
### Parametric estimating
- Mathematical modeling tool for cost estimation
- similar to analogous top-down
- better to be used with historical info
### Vector Bid analysis
- This method estimates the cost based on the amount of bids the vendor makes
### Reserve Analysis
- used to determine if the amount of remaining reserve left is adequate
- compares the amount of risk remaining at any time in project
## Typical problems in IT cost estimation
- estimates are done too quickly
- people lack estimating experience
- human beings are biased towards underestimation
- management desires accuracy
# Earned Value Management (EVM)
- project management methodology that integrates schedule, costs, and scope to measure project performance.
- we must provide actual information periodically to use EVM.
- Earned value analysis is an approach for measuring how much work has been completed in a project at a given point of time and performance.
## Basis
1. Planned Value (PV)
	- budgeted cost of work schedule (BCWS)
	- authorized budget assigned to schedule work
	- PV = total project cost * percent of planned work
	- PV for complete project for 5 months = $25,000
	- PV at 2 months = 2/5 * 25,000 = $10,000
2. Actual Cost (AC)
	- actual cost of work performed (ACWP)
	- real cost associated with the work done on an activitiy
	- lets assume, AC at the end of 2 months = $15,000
3. earned value (EV)
	- budgeted cost of work performed (BCWP)
	- measure of work performed expressed in terms of budget authorized for that work.
	- EV can't be greater than the authorized PV budget.
	- lets say you managed to finish 30% of the work.
	- EV = total project cost * % of actual work = $25,000 * 30\% - $7,500
4. Cost Variance (CV)
	- difference between Budgeted cost of Work Performed (EV) and Actual Cost of Work Performed (AC)
	- CV = BCWP - ACWP
5. Schedule Variance (SV)
	- difference of budget cost of work performed (EV) and Budgeted Cost of work schedule (PV)
	- SV = BCWP - BCWS
	- if negative, it indicates that cost are overrun
6. Rate of Performance (RP)
	- ratio of actual work completed to the percentage of work palnned to have been completed.
	- is = $\large \dfrac{\text{Actual work completed}}{\text{\% of work planned}}$
## Benefits
1. EVM is an excellent measure of progress
2. enables accurate forecasting
3. supports management by exception
4. promotes good project management disciplines
5. cost effective
