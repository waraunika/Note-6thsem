# 1 Program Management
- A **program** is a group of related projects managed in a coordinated way to obtain benefits and control that are not available from managing them individually.
## 1.1 Aspects of Program Management
- **Schedule Integration:** Projects are scheduled on a master program plan to optimize resource utilization and sequencing.
- **Dependency Management:** Managing interdependencies between projects (e.g., one project's output becomes another project's input).
- **Holistic Governance:** The program is managed as a single entity with its own project management tasks, including:
    - Cost management
    - Risk management (at the program level)
    - Resource allocation across projects
    - Communication and stakeholder management
    - Quality assurance
## 1.2 Program Management in IT Context
- **Large-Scale IT Initiatives:** Breaking down large, complex IT projects into a set of smaller, related projects managed under a common program management scope.
- **Enterprise Architecture Governance:** Managing the enterprise IT architecture and ensuring its consistent and effective use across all projects within the program.
# 2 Portfolio
- A **portfolio** is a collection of projects, programs, and other work that are grouped together to facilitate effective management and meet strategic business objectives.
## 2.1 Project Portfolio Management
- is a strategy that enables organizations to align their projects, programs, resources, and initiatives with corporate business objectives. It treats IT assets as financial assets and runs projects in a project-oriented business manner.
- PPM enables integrated management of:
	- Pipeline and scope
	- Time, resources, and skills
	- Cost, procurement, and communication
	- Reporting, forecasting, and risk management
### 2.1.1 Analogy
- Just as a financial portfolio diversifies investments (stocks, bonds, real estate) to balance risk and maximize returns, a project portfolio balances different types of projects to:
	- **Reduce risk:** Spreading investments across high-risk/high-reward and low-risk/stable projects.
	- **Optimize returns:** Selecting projects that collectively deliver the greatest business value.
	- **Maintain balance:** Ensuring a healthy mix of strategic, growth, and maintenance projects.
### 2.1.2 Why Project Portfolio Management
| Problem                                  | Description                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Misalignment with Business**           | Business managers often lack understanding of how IT can help achieve corporate goals; IT is sometimes viewed as a "necessary evil."                                     |
| **Failure to Deliver Value**             | Even when IT investment is recognized as important, projects often fail to deliver value because they are not aligned with business objectives.                          |
| **Overcommitment & Poor Prioritization** | Organizations launch more projects than they can handle effectively and fail to set priorities based on business objectives, leading to resource bottlenecks and delays. |
### 2.1.3 Key benefits of PPM
|Benefit|Description|
|---|---|
|**Closer Alignment of IT with Business**|Provides a holistic view of the entire project portfolio, enabling executives to understand where IT dollars are spent and which projects continue to deliver value.|
|**Better IT Governance**|Enables real-time monitoring of project progress and provides detailed data to support compliance requirements (e.g., Sarbanes-Oxley Act).|
|**Cost Reductions & Productivity Increases**|Helps identify redundancies, allocate resources appropriately, make better staffing/outsourcing decisions, and spot opportunities for asset reuse.|
|**Business-Based Decision Making**|Allows managers to make decisions based not only on projected costs but also on anticipated risks and returns in relation to other projects in the portfolio.|
|**More Predictable Project Outcomes**|Bridges the gap between business managers and project practitioners, ensures consistent processes, and helps assess project status in real time.|
### 2.1.4 Types of PPM Users
| User Type                       | Focus                                 | Key Activities                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Execution-Focused PPM Users** | Tactical details of project execution | Managing day-to-day project activities; using reporting tools to communicate progress and expenditures to business sponsors and executive management.                      |
| **Portfolio-Level PPM Users**   | Strategic decision-making             | Creating project selection frameworks; selecting projects based on those frameworks; planning and tracking investments at a high level; reporting on portfolio activities. |
# 3 Project Management Office
## 3.1 Definition
- A **Project Management Office (PMO)** is a group or department within an organization that defines and maintains standards for project management.
- The PMO strives to standardize processes and introduce economies of repetition in the execution of projects.
- **Key Functions:**
	- Source of documentation, guidance, and metrics for project management practices.
	- Establishes and maintains procedures and standards for project management methodologies.
	- In some organizations, this is called a **Program Management Office (PgMO)** , with a subtle difference:
		- program management focuses on governing a set of related projects.
## 3.2 The case for PMO
- According to the **Standish CHAOS Report (2009)** :
	- **68%** of projects do not meet time, cost, and scope targets.
	- Only **32%** of projects were completed on time, within budget, and delivered measurable business and stakeholder benefits.
- A **KPMG survey** of 252 organizations revealed that technology is not the most critical factor in project failure:
	- **32%** of failures attributed to inadequate project management implementation.
	- **17%** attributed to unfamiliarity with scope and complexity.
	- **Conclusion:** Approximately **69%** of project failures are due to a lack of or improper implementation of project management methodologies.
## 3.3 Types of PMO
| Type                                  | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Enterprise PMO**                    | Services the entire organization, focusing on corporate and strategic issues. May manage projects for cost reduction efforts or strategic initiatives.                                                            |
| **Organizational (Departmental) PMO** | Operates within a specific functional area or division (e.g., Information Systems). Primary responsibility is managing a critical resource pool and resource allocation. May or may not directly manage projects. |
| **Special-Purpose PMO**               | Established for a specific purpose, such as managing a customer group or a temporary initiative. Multiple special-purpose PMOs can exist simultaneously.                                                          |
### 3.3.1 Alternative Categorization
| Type                          | Focus                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Functional PMO**            | Manages a resource pool within one functional area; focuses on resource management rather than project management.                                            |
| **Corporate (Strategic) PMO** | Services the entire company; focuses on corporate and strategic issues, not functional ones.                                                                  |
| **Customer Group PMO**        | Clusters common customers or projects for better management and customer relations; may function as a temporary organization with dedicated project managers. |
## 3.4 Functions of PMO
| Function Category                 | Specific Functions                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Standardization**               | Estimating, planning, scheduling, control, reporting; clarifying roles and responsibilities.                                                                            |
| **Methodology & Best Practices**  | Developing templates, methodologies, and best practices; recommending and implementing improvements to existing standards.                                              |
| **Knowledge Management**          | Preparing archive of lessons learned; continuous benchmarking; acting as guardian for project management intellectual property; establishing a problem-solving hotline. |
| **Training & Development**        | Coordinating and conducting training programs; transferring knowledge through coaching and mentorship.                                                                  |
| **Strategic & Resource Planning** | Performing strategic planning for project management; developing corporate resource capacity/utilization plans.                                                         |
| **Risk & Portfolio Management**   | Assessing project risks; planning for disaster recovery; participating in portfolio management.                                                                         |
# 4 Drivers of Project Success
| Success Driver                  | Description                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Top Management Support**      | Very strong and visible commitment from top management; CIO is always ready to help.   |
| **Clear Goals & Objectives**    | Well-defined, understood, and agreed-upon project objectives.                          |
| **Client Support**              | Active involvement and commitment from the client or key stakeholders.                 |
| **Realistic Plan**              | A well-developed, achievable plan that accounts for risks and uncertainties.           |
| **Appropriate Resources**       | Adequate availability of the **4Ms**: Man, Machine, Material, Money.                   |
| **Ownership**                   | Clear accountability and a sense of ownership among team members.                      |
| **Formal Methodology**          | Use of a structured, consistent project management approach.                           |
| **Hardworking, Focused Staff**  | A dedicated team committed to project success.                                         |
| **Structured ICT Support**      | Standard and well-functioning information and communication technology infrastructure. |
| **Effective Communication**     | Open, timely, and clear communication across all stakeholders.                         |
| **Experienced Project Manager** | A skilled project manager with relevant technical and leadership experience.           |
# 5 Inhibitors of project success
| Inhibitor                        | Description                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Poor Communication**           | Lack of clear, timely, and effective communication among stakeholders.                      |
| **Lack of Leadership**           | Absence of strong, decisive, and supportive leadership.                                     |
| **Unclear Expectations**         | Ambiguous or conflicting stakeholder expectations regarding project outcomes.               |
| **Poor Upfront Planning**        | Inadequate planning at the start of the project, leading to later issues.                   |
| **Negative Attitude Towards IT** | Inbuilt skepticism or resistance toward IT projects and their value.                        |
| **Changing Business Strategies** | Frequent shifts in organizational priorities and strategic direction.                       |
| **Poor Top Management Support**  | Lack of visible commitment, resources, and backing from senior leadership.                  |
| **Conflicts of Objectives**      | Divergent or competing goals among stakeholders or departments.                             |
| **Inadequate Resources**         | Insufficient staff, budget, equipment, or time to complete the project.                     |
| **Financial Limitations**        | Budget constraints or funding cuts during the project lifecycle.                            |
| **Lack of Historical Data**      | No access to past project data, lessons learned, or benchmarks for planning and estimation. |
